<summary><b>Week 0: Optional review</b></summary>

<br>
Welcome to the course! 

If it's been a little since you've done any calculus, it's probably a good idea to practice some of the fundamental skills from Calc I: differentiating and integrating functions. 

I recommend trying your hand at some of the following exercises from our Calc I textbook: 

- [<b>Product, quotient, and power rule:</b>](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/3-3-differentiation-rules) Try 106 through 117 of the exercises to chapter 3.3. 
- [<b>Trig derivatives:</b>](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/3-5-derivatives-of-trigonometric-functions) Try 175 through 184 of the exercises to chapter 3.5. 
- [<b>Chain rule:</b>](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/3-6-the-chain-rule) Try 220 through 237 of the exercises to chapter 3.6. 
- [<b>Definite integrals:</b>](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/5-3-the-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus) Try 170 through 189 of the exercises to chapter 5.3. 
- [<b>Substitution:</b>](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/5-5-substitution) Try 261 through 287 of the exercises to chaper 5.5. 

<br><b>You certainly don't need to do all of these,</b> but I want you to come to Calc II feeling confident! This means you should do as many of these as you need to refresh your memory and feel comfortable with each kind of question. Note that the textbook offers answers to the odd-numbered problems at the end. These aren't *solutions*---you still have to figure out how to get the answer yourself---but they give you a way to check whether you've done a problem correctly. 

In addition to the above, you can also get started reading new material from the textbook. The way GW sets up its Calculus classes delays some topics typically taught in Calc I to Calc II, so we'll spend the first week going over some material from the Calc I textbook before we switch over to the Calc II textbook. Specifically, our first topic will be inverse functions, which is chapter 1.4 of the Calc I textbook. 

<b>Finally, everyone should read over the syllabus before the first day of class.</b>

</details>


Note: Sections in the OpenStax Calc I textbook have a '0.' prefix. E.g. 'OpenStax 0.3.7' refers to '3.7 Derivatives of Inverse Funtions' from the Calc I textbook, whereas 'OpenStax 3.7' refers to '3.7 Improper Integrals' from the Calc II textbook.
